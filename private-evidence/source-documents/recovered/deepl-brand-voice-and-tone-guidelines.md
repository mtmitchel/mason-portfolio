Purpose of voice and tone guidelines
What are “voice” and “tone”?
It’s not what you say, it’s how you say it. Voice and tone are laser-focused on the how.
Our voice is the overarching way we communicate. It unites all our brand communication, and in doing so, creates a linguistic signature that builds trust with our partners and
customers.
Our tone is how we apply our voice across different channels. It’s what allows us to flex that linguistic signature across all the applications we need to.
What is “brand communication”?
Voice and tone focus on brand communication.
That’s things like website copy, product copy, social media posts, press releases, and customer service messages.

Who is this guide for?
Do you write and work at DeepL? Then this guide is for you. Okay, yes. We know that means everyone. Let’s be more specific.
This guide is helpful for everyone who works at DeepL or writes on behalf of DeepL, but it’s particularly useful for copywriting, marketing, PR, product, and customer service teams, as well as
external content creators.
Our brand idea
Let’s start at the beginning. Even before we dive into how we write, we need to take a look at the idea that drives all our writing—powering better expression.
When you’re writing for DeepL, use “better expression” as your North Star. It is central to all our communications, for all our audiences.
Defining “better expression”
Together, “better expression” positions our products as tools that enable and enhance how individuals and businesses communicate with the world.
“Better expression” in evergreen copy
It’s not things like research papers or private conversations, although some of the tips in this guide might be useful for those, too.
When we say better, we mean:
Effective and top-quality (delivered through Write & Translator)
Multinational (delivered through Translator)
Fully secure (delivered through paid plans for Write, Translator, and the API)
Personalized (delivered through the glossary feature, style and tone options, and with LLM
interactivity coming soon)
When we say expression, we mean:
Finding the right words, at the right time
Fostering connections between people, across languages
Increasing productivity across teams
Fostering growth, without borders or limits

Personality
According to the Aaker brand personality model, all brands fall into five categories. These are sincerity, competence, excitement, sophistication, and ruggedness. Personality is defined by our
traits and characteristics.
When using this lens to explore our own personality, one word stood out. DeepL is, and has always been, a competent brand.
Our brand line is designed to be aspirational, not exact. It is used as a sign-off for applications at the top of the funnel. This includes things like out-of-home (OOH) advertisements or
videos. It can also be used on the level of social media bios or as a homepage message.
Our brand line does not need to be used in more descriptive applications, like product copy or Help Center copy.

Voice
DeepL’s voice is static and high-level. It encompasses both the personality and tone, remaining consistent across all communications.
There are four characteristics that make up our voice. These are present in all our brand communications.
Helpful
We’re helpful to our users, providing them with only the necessary information. Keep it brief, explain yourself clearly. Before you write something, ask yourself “does the user care about this?” If
they do, keep it in. If they don’t, cut it. Yes, we build impressive products that outshine our competitors in terms of quality. But no matter how complex our technology is, we explain it quickly and
simply, guiding users to their desired goal or intent.
Empathetic
We solve language-centric problems. The type of problems that everybody faces. We all know what it feels like to not understand someone else’s language or to be unsure how to articulate
ourselves. That’s why we approach our customers with warmth and empathy.
We always put our users first, focusing on business benefits for enterprise buyers and getting more personal with our end users. This means writing more about the customer than about DeepL
(more “you/your,” less “we/us/our”). This also includes using colloquialisms sometimes, and getting straight to the point so the reader is always able to find what they need and care about. It also
helps to acknowledge customer pain points and offer solutions and responses that speak to those.
Self-assured
We’re pioneers of Language AI. Our products are built by some of the best minds in AI, ML, and linguistics. But we’re not a research arm or a scientific lab. Instead, we’re a product company.
We write for the people we serve, not our peers in the AI/ML space. That takes self-assuredness.
We build Language AI that benefits people and businesses, so we write with them in mind. That means always positioning our technology in service of people, rather than falling into deep
scientific descriptions or overly complicated explanations. And it means having the confidence to own our technological term, Language AI—say it loud, and say it proud.
Professional
We’re in the business of language, and it’s a serious job. Misunderstandings cost businesses and people countless hours (and money, too). Our users need to trust us, and that’s why we use a
tone that’s courteous and friendly but not overly excited. That said, having a professional tone doesn’t have to sound cold or clinical.

communication. Some channels require a more professional tone, while some can be more empathetic. Below is a breakdown of when to use which tone of voice.

Be a leader in AI
We’ve changed the landscape of Language AI. Our team includes some of the best minds in AI/ML and linguistics. Don’t be afraid to call this out. Prioritize using our phrase “Language AI” when
you can, or use “AI” on its own when it feels appropriate.
Get specific about solutions
Our platform can be used across industries, locations, and use cases. But to connect with our users, we need to address their specific use cases and pain points. Don’t be afraid to call out
particular features, products, or instances where DeepL is beneficial, even in very detailed contexts.
Don’t just say it, prove it
Anyone can say that they do things “better”; we prove it. To do that, we share stats, customer stories, and quotes in our communications. We use copy in illustrations to show not only how
DeepL works, but why it works. We share case studies which illustrate the results that we delivered, not just the products we provided.
Write with clarity
Clarity creates understanding. When we’re clear with our messaging, our customers and users know what we do, which in turn allows them to better express themselves when using our
products. Be sure to always communicate with focus and clarity when writing.

Our audiences
Decision makers (e.g., CEOs, CIOs, etc.)
CEOs or CIOs: people who don’t need complicated descriptions of our technology or jargon-filled explanations of our products. People who need to know what we can do for them at a business
level. When targeting CIOs/IT decision makers, emphasize security and trust, speak precisely about the products, and cut out “marketing fluff.”
Remember: many decision-makers won’t know exactly what DeepL does. Show them through tangible examples that connect to the goals of almost every company—increased revenue and
decreased costs. The return on investment (ROI) should always be clear, and we should be anticipating their questions about what DeepL’s AI can deliver.
End users: (new and existing)
New users: think anyone who is new to DeepL and may not know about our brand and products. New users need to be educated both about our brand and products and should be able to
easily understand how DeepL can deliver on its brand promise of enabling better expression through our products and messaging.
Existing users: think anyone who already uses DeepL products, understands our brand, and already trusts us. We continue to provide them value through the idea of better expression and
help them achieve more in their professional lives through our products. Existing and long-time users are our brand champions; therefore, we need to continuously build a relationship with them
and give them reasons to believe.
Potential customers
Think professionals who need translation services or writing support and a reliable solution to help them.
Remember that all our potential users are people, and people connect with stories. Don’t hesitate to include details in specific use cases that connect to real experiences. After you’ve drawn
them in, give them the reasons to choose DeepL—quality, speed, security, and flexibility.
How do we talk about. . .?
Competitors
Lean into our self-assured tone by leading with the facts. Place our principles “Be a leader in AI” and “Don’t just say it, prove it” at the forefront in the way we write about competitors. Don’t be
afraid to use bold examples, as long as they are backed up by facts.
Point out DeepL’s differentiators without competitor-bashing. This can be achieved by communicating:
What the difference is (e.g., “Language experts prefer DeepL over GPT-4, Microsoft, and Google in blind tests.”)
How and why DeepL is different (e.g., “DeepL’s LLM is specialized, runs on proprietary data, and is tutored by human language experts.”)
Why it matters to the customer: bring it back to real benefits and impact (e.g., time saved, ROI)

Good news, celebrations, milestones
Lean into our self-assured tone by leading with the facts. Let other people do the cheering for us by creating spaces for other voices to shout about us (e.g., customer quotes).
Bad news, setbacks
Face these head-on by highlighting our empathetic tone. Do not hide bad news in a piece of communications that is about something else. Make sure to remind our customers and users that our
great differentiator is quality, and that no matter the setback, we will continue to pursue this above all else.
Legal and data privacy matters
Utilize our professional tone here, giving our readers the information they need quickly and efficiently.
