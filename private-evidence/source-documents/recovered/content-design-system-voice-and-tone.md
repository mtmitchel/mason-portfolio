Voice and tone (Maddie)
Personality
According to the Aaker brand personality model, DeepL is a competent brand.
Why competent?
● Because of our abilities
● Because we’re driven by a desire to be useful
● Because we leave our ego at the door
● Because we’re easy to understand
Voice
DeepL’s voice is static and high-level. It remains consistent across all communications.
There are four characteristics that make up our voice: helpful, empathetic, self-assured, and
professional. These are present in all our brand communications.
Helpful
We’re helpful to our users, providing them with only the necessary information. Keep it brief;
explain yourself clearly. Before you write something, ask yourself “does the user care about
this?” If they do, keep it in. If they don’t, cut it. No matter how complex our technology is, we
explain it quickly and simply, guiding users to their desired goal or intent.
Empathetic
We always put our users first. This means writing more about them than about DeepL (more
“you/your,” less “we/us/our”) and taking the time to analyze and acknowledge the emotional
state that they are in. This also includes using colloquialisms sometimes and getting straight
to the point so the reader is always able to find what they need and care about. It also helps
to acknowledge customer pain points and offer solutions and responses that speak to those.
Self-assured
We build Language AI that benefits people and businesses, so we write with them in mind.
That means always positioning our technology in service of people, rather than falling into
deep scientific descriptions or overly complicated explanations. And it means having the
confidence to own our technological term, Language AI—say it loud, and say it proud.
Professional
We’re in the business of language, and it’s a serious job. Misunderstandings cost businesses
and people countless hours (and money, too). Our users need to trust us, and that’s why we
use a tone that’s courteous and friendly but not overly excited. That said, having a
professional tone doesn’t have to sound cold or clinical.

Tone
The four characteristics that make up DeepL’s voice combine in different ways to create a
tone that suits the context of the communication. Below is a breakdown of tone by situation
for UX content.
Announcements
We use a helpful and professional tone for announcements. Announcements are often
related to new products, plans, features, or releases. Our aim is to provide our users with
only the context and detail they need, getting straight to the point whenever possible. Our
focus is always on how the latest developments can benefit them and their pain points rather
than on what DeepL has accomplished.
Do Frame the announcement with jobs-to-be-done and user pain points in mind.
Don’t Make DeepL the focus of the announcement.
Celebrations
Celebrations are a chance for us to create small moments of accomplishment for our users
through an encouraging and empathetic tone. There are two ends of the spectrum for

celebrations: for small wins, we use a short and sweet approach. For bigger milestones, we
can turn up the excitement, but this should be done sparingly and reserved for situations that
truly deserve their own moment.
Do Highlight users quick wins like “Nice work! You translated 25 documents this month.”
Don’t Make small moments bigger than they are like “You translated 500 words. You’re a
superstar!”
Error messages
Errors are a frustrating situation for our users. We use a helpful and empathetic tone. We are
clear and direct, making sure they have a general understanding of what happened and
what they can do to fix it.
Do Explain the error in the simplest way possible.
Do Whenever possible, include short instructions on how to fix the error.
Don’t Use technical jargon or vague language like “You can’t perform this action.”
Don’t Apologize for simple errors like “Sorry, that isn’t a valid email.”
Learning and educating
When educating users, we use a helpful and professional tone. We are often onboarding
them to new products or features or increasing adoption to existing features that they might
not be aware of. Similar to announcements, we want to ensure they can easily find the
information that is relevant to them. This copy should inspire confidence and clarify what
exactly they can gain from the information.
Do When there’s a lot of information, break things down into digestible chunks.

Don’t Overexplain obvious things. We don’t have to spell out every detail for our users,
like which button to click when there is only one primary action.
