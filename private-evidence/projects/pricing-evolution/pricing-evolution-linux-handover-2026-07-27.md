## Mason's writing feedback

The following points are requirements, not optional preferences. Derogatory
language from the live feedback is omitted; its force and meaning are
preserved.

### Meaning and precision

- `A pricing grid should make one decision legible: what changes when someone
  moves up a plan?` calls a question a decision. Mason's point: words must name
  the thing they actually mean.
- Do not use adjacent abstractions as though they are parallel actions.
  `The work required editing as well as hierarchy` mismatches a verb-like
  activity with a noun and says nothing concrete.
- `Served different reading moments` is unnatural, vague English. State who did
  what, what changed, and why it mattered.
- Do not substitute a label such as `working proposal` for the actual work or
  its consequence.

### Positioning and confidence

- A hiring case must show what Mason did. `I helped reshape a working proposal`
  hides both the action and the shipped change.
- The cumulative change is visible on DeepL's current pricing page. The
  sentence `This remained a working proposal rather than a shipped result` was
  false.
- The draft was overcautious because it treated missing file-level proof as a
  reason to suppress Mason's account and a visible product state.
- Evidence limits must prevent exaggeration without erasing the strongest
  supported positive statement.
- The prose must have confidence, clarity, and conviction. Confidence here
  means using the strongest defensible action and lifecycle claim, not
  inventing sole ownership or results.

### Story selection

- Do not mention Write if the case does not unpack it.
- Do not mention a detailed comparison table if the reader never sees it.
- Do not import Voice or API to make the project appear larger.
- Every artifact and every secondary product must advance the exact story on
  the page.
- The draft must show a completed change, not turn a case study into an
  inventory of drafts and caveats.

### Headings and narrative movement

- The rejected headings read like internal state notes.
- A heading must be engaging and must advance the narrative. It should carry a
  concrete fact, turn, tension, choice, or consequence.
- Claude's later proposed headings were also rejected as objectively poor.
  They are listed below only so they are not accidentally reused:
  `Double the price, the same eight lines`, `Only what the plan adds`,
  `The ladder went missing`, and `Three lines instead of eight`.
- Do not generate replacement headings by mechanically applying a template.
  Study strong published portfolio examples and write headings for the actual
  story.

Mason rejected both of these passages and does not want similar prose:

> This remained a working proposal rather than a shipped result. It also made
> a residual question easier to see: Ultimate was roughly twice the Advanced

## Independent web research

The following live sources were checked because Mason required web research
before any headline or writing-skill changes.

### Portfolio examples and hiring advice

- [Daniel McLeod](https://danielmcleod.ca/) uses a specific case title,
  `Scaling understanding of a complex credit model`, and pairs it with an
  outcome-led summary.
- [Simon Pan](https://simonpan.com/) leads with work and results. His case
  headings act as narrative turns rather than document labels, including
  `Structuring Content First` and `Thinking Big, Starting Small`.
- [UX Content Collective hiring and portfolio advice](https://uxcontent.com/get-hired-job-hunting-and-portfolio-advice-for-content-designers/)
  recommends framing cases around the problem rather than the task, using
  cause-and-effect movement, making the individual's role clear, and explaining
  why decisions were made.

These examples do not provide approved Pricing Evolution headings. They show
the standard to meet: specificity, point of view, visible consequence, and
narrative movement.

### Headings and sentence craft

- [GOV.UK clear titles](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-titles/)
  says a title must work by itself, use common language, choose the right verb,
  and describe the content clearly.
- [ONS plain language](https://service-manual.ons.gov.uk/content/writing-for-users/plain-language)
  recommends front-loading the words that describe the user's task and using
  simple headings.
- [GOV.UK clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
  recommends active voice, short sentences, and short paragraphs.
- [Microsoft writing tips](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/global-communications/writing-tips)
  recommends subject–verb–object order, active indicative verbs, consistent
  terminology, and avoiding modifier stacks and overlinked clauses.
- [Microsoft's top writing tips](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/top-10-tips-style-voice)
  recommends leading with the important information, expressing bigger ideas
  in fewer words, reading prose aloud, pruning, and avoiding empty
  `there is/there are` openings.
- [Purdue OWL on concision](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/graduate_writing/documents/IWE-2020-Writing-Concisely.pdf)
  explains how nominalizations hide actions and recommends cutting redundant
  conjunctions, prepositional strings, inflated phrases, and unnecessary tense
  complexity.

Together these sources support rules against relational filler, hidden verbs,
abstract noun stacks, vague pronouns, unparallel constructions, and headings
that merely label a section.

## Positioning note that must survive the operating-system switch

Mason supplied a Windows-only attachment defining the professional requirement
as **positioning**: take the strongest defensible interpretation of the
experience and express it in the language of the target role.

The important content is:

- Work backward from what a hiring manager needs to see.
- Select, translate, emphasize, and compress instead of producing a neutral
  inventory.
- Turn duties into accomplishments.
- Show scope, difficulty, stakeholders, or complexity when available.
- Translate ordinary tasks into the professional capability they demonstrate.
- Use the strongest reasonable verb. `Led`, `owned`, and `drove` are valid only
  when the actual role supports their normal meanings.
- Ask: What changed? What did I personally do? How difficult or large was it?
  Why did it matter?
- Write: strong verb + valuable activity + scope or complexity + result or
  business purpose.
- Describe the work's value from the employer's viewpoint, not the labor from
  the worker's viewpoint.

For this case, positioning does not mean claiming sole ownership or metrics. It
means leading with the supported shipped contribution instead of leading with
the limits of the source files.

### `build-content-design-portfolio`

Add portfolio-specific requirements for:

1. **Positioning**
   - State the strongest supported action, change, scope, and hiring value.
   - Do not let an evidence caveat become the case's thesis.

2. **Evidence floor and ceiling**
   - The ceiling blocks unsupported ownership, lifecycle, causation, or
     outcomes.
   - The floor requires every supported positive fact that materially changes
     the reader's understanding.
   - Missing metadata is not positive evidence that work did not ship.

3. **Lifecycle verification**
   - Never write `did not ship`, `remained a proposal`, `was not adopted`, or
     equivalent language unless a source positively establishes that status.
   - When current product state matters and can change, check the current
     product before drafting.
   - Keep working-artifact identity separate from whether the underlying change
     shipped.

4. **Artifact-to-thesis fit**
   - If the prose makes a table, flow, or screen central, the reader must see
     it.
   - Cut unsupported secondary products and process artifacts.
   - Every image must prove or advance one narrative claim.

5. **Hiring-reader gate**
   - A reader must be able to state the problem, Mason's action, the judgment,
     what changed, and why it matters after one quick read.
   - A mechanical pass cannot override explicit user rejection.
   - The independent reader must not be the agent or session that wrote or
     repaired the draft.

Specific acceptance tests:

- Reject `I helped reshape a working proposal` when the evidence supports a
  more direct action and a shipped change.
- Reject `The plan cards and detailed comparison table served different
  reading moments` when the table is not shown and the sentence does not state
  a concrete job.
- Reject any narrative that uses absence of source metadata to write a negative
  lifecycle claim.
- Reject a case whose strongest fact appears only in an evidence note or
  caption.

### `narrative-writer`

Add long-form craft and revision requirements for:

1. **Concrete action**
   - Prefer actor + action + object + consequence.
   - Replace relational verbs and nominalizations when a specific action verb
     exists.
   - Keep grammatical comparisons parallel.

2. **Narrative headings**
   - A heading must carry a concrete fact, tension, choice, turn, or
     consequence.
   - Reject internal-state labels, generic process labels, and headings that
     require the paragraph below them to become meaningful.
   - Judge headings in sequence: they should form a compressed version of the
     story.

3. **Plain expository English**
   - Reject modifier stacks, strings of abstract nouns, vague pronouns,
     overlinked clauses, empty `there is/there are` openings, and phrases such
     as `served different reading moments`.
   - Use short subject–verb–object sentences where complexity does not require
     another structure.
   - Read the prose aloud and revise any sentence that sounds translated,
     bureaucratic, or evasive.

4. **Endings**
   - End on the achieved change, its consequence, or the demonstrated judgment.
   - Reject `I would carry forward`, `would need validation`, generic lessons,
     and cautious self-assessment when the story has an actual completed
     result.
   - Do not introduce an unseen artifact or a new unresolved issue in the
     ending.

Specific acceptance tests:

- Reject `The work required editing as well as hierarchy`; it hides the actor
  and action and uses unmatched grammatical forms.
- Reject a sentence that calls a question a decision.
- Reject a heading that only names a drafting stage.
- Reject a closing paragraph that could be copied into an unrelated case
  without changing its meaning.
