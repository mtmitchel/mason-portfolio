## Agreements

### The candidate has a real and legible spine

Both readers found the opening unusually effective. It states the problem,
Mason's collaborative action, the restored cue, shipment, and current
relevance before asking the reader to invest in the details.

Both identified `Six lines came off Ultimate, and one line had to go back` as
the strongest heading and the story's genuine turn. It shows Mason recognizing
that removing repetition can also remove meaning.

### The evidence mostly advances the story

Both readers found the first image essential. The repeated Advanced and
Ultimate lists and the two changed limits are visible without relying on a
claim of measured impact.

Both found the second image useful evidence for the cumulative structure and
restored inheritance cue. Neither recommended replacing it or adding another
product. The red hand-annotated image remains excluded.

### One accessibility instruction is visibly wrong

Both independently rejected Image 2's direction to `Convey that lists shorten
as the price rises`. The visible lists do not shorten consistently: they grow
from Free to Starter and again from Ultimate to Enterprise. The image supports
the narrower statement that each paid tier isolates its additions.

This is a defect, not a stylistic preference.

### The prose and ending have hiring value

Both readers found the prose mostly direct, concrete, and free of inflated
strategy language. Both valued the before-and-after strings as visible craft
evidence and the refusal to invent metrics.

Both found the final principle confident and specific: a card can remove what
the reader already has only while it still says what the reader is getting.
Both also found factual seams in the closing paragraph that must be considered
before approval.

## Disagreements and different emphases

### Is the first heading safely separate from the rejected framing?

- **Codex:** `€49.99 repeated everything €24.99 already said` is new prose but
 conceptually close to the prohibited rejected heading `Double the price, the
 same eight lines`. That weakens the blank-page separation.
- **Claude:** The heading set is one of the candidate's strongest features and
 should remain unchanged.

Claude was intentionally not shown the rejected headings or archived draft, so
it could not assess recovery separation. Its positive reaction establishes
reader value, not independence from the rejected framing. Mason must decide
whether the echo is too close.

### Does the inheritance heading fully solve the thin-card problem?

- **Codex:** The restored cue is clear evidence of judgment and the strongest
 turn.
- **Claude:** Image 2 still leaves Ultimate visibly emptier than the other paid
 cards, so the heading may only partly solve the visual impression that it is
 the smallest offer.

The disagreement concerns visual sufficiency, not whether the cue mattered.
The missing-cue state is not shown, and the prohibited red image must not be
added to solve that gap.

### Does cumulative language create a limits-versus-features ambiguity?

- **Claude:** `All Advanced features, plus: 100 file translations/month` can
 read as if the inherited 20-file limit and the new 100-file limit both
 apply. Features accumulate, but limits replace lower limits. Claude saw this
 as the highest-value unresolved content-design question.
- **Codex:** Did not identify this as a quick-read failure; the higher limits
 read as ordinary tier upgrades.

This needs Mason's product and project context before any repair. The comparison
does not assume the pattern failed, invent a user problem, or authorize adding
an explanation unsupported by the work.

### How much of the opening diagnosis belongs in this story?

- **Codex:** The Enterprise vocabulary and line-wrapping detour delay Mason's
 action and risk turning a focused contribution into a broad grid audit.
- **Claude:** Did not recommend cutting the Enterprise material, but treated
 missing stakes and precise idea ownership as possible senior-level gaps.

Both point to positioning rather than factual safety: the current narrative may
spend space on secondary diagnosis while leaving organizational pressure and
the exact origin of the cumulative rule implicit.

### Is the fifth column a supported consequence?

- **Codex:** `A fifth column fit into the same grid` sits beside the content
 reductions and can imply that Mason's change caused the fifth tier to fit.
 The two images have different dimensions and are not a controlled
 comparison.
- **Claude:** Repeated the fifth-column statement in its restatement and did
 not flag the causal implication. It separately warned that the polished
 images can read as a clean before/after even though the second also adds Free
 and rewrites Enterprise.

The safer conclusion is that the second working state visibly contains five
tiers. The material does not prove that the content change caused the fifth
tier to fit.

## Findings raised by only one reader

1. `Most ran to two or three rows` is contradicted by Image 1; only a minority
 of the benefit lines wrap.
2. `The lines themselves slowed the comparison down` and `the eye had to
 track` state reading effects more strongly than the available rationale

 and obscures the concrete interface problem.
2. `The same benefit should read the same way in every column` conflicts on a
 quick read with a structure designed to stop repeating benefits across
 columns. The intended claim appears to be consistent terminology and
 sentence shape across the grid.
3. The nine-item private claim note is materially longer than the requested
 short preservation note and could eventually combine related boundaries.
