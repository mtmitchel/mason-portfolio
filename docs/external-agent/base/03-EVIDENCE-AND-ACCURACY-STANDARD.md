# Evidence and accuracy standard

Use evidence to prevent material falsehoods, not to flatten the story.

The evidence library is a set of related goals, initiatives, artifacts,
decisions, outcomes, and leads. It helps discover strong combinations; it does
not dictate the case title, outline, visual hierarchy, or current structure.

## Keep four questions separate

1. **Artifact:** What work or working material exists?
2. **Contribution:** What did Mason do?
3. **Lifecycle:** Was the work exploratory, approved, tested, launched,
   changed, or retired?
4. **Outcome:** What qualitative or quantitative result is supported?

Evidence for one question does not automatically answer another. A screenshot
does not prove authorship. A metric does not prove that one writer caused it.
An unknown lifecycle is not proof that something did not ship.

## Use confident language at the supported level

- State Mason’s documented actions and reasoning directly.
- Mason is a primary source for his own role, participation, contribution,
  intent, rationale, and recollection.
- State intent as intent. Do not turn it into tested user behavior.
- Attribute team, experiment, program, or campaign outcomes at that level.
- Do not imply sole ownership or personal causation unless the case packet
  supports it.
- External product research can clarify public product history, terminology,
  or market context. It cannot establish Mason's role, a private team goal, or
  project causation.

## Professional analysis is not fabrication

Agents may extrapolate and explain what a UX, microcopy, or content-design
change would help a user do when Figma mockups, Mason's recollection, or other
artifacts support the reading and established professional practice supports
the reasoning. Use that tradecraft in case-study rationales and explanations,
and cite the relevant best practice or public standard when useful. Keep the
inferred user benefit distinct from a claim that the project observed or
measured that benefit.

For example, when a Figma comparison uses consistent plan names and parallel
headings, it is valid to explain that the structure would help users compare
plans and find relevant differences with less effort, based on information
hierarchy, scannability, and recognition-over-recall practice. Project-specific
testing or a conversion metric is not required for that professional analysis.

## Facts, leads, and gaps

Keep these categories distinct:

- **Supported fact:** may be used at the scope described in the packet.
- **Professional interpretation:** a user benefit or rationale extrapolated
  from artifacts and established practice.
- **Discovery lead:** useful for finding a stronger source or story
  relationship, but not ready for a public claim.
- **Material gap:** missing information that changes whether the story can be
  told honestly.

Do not convert a discovery lead into public certainty. Do not turn harmless
missing metadata into a material gap.

## Representative visuals

A representative working artifact can show the product area, flow, or content
problem Mason worked on. It does not need an unbroken file-to-production chain
unless the public copy calls it the exact tested, winning, approved, shipped,
or production version.

Describe the image according to what it is. Do not make its internal provenance
the public story.

## Evidence floor and ceiling

The **ceiling** removes or qualifies claims the evidence cannot support.

The **floor** requires the writer to include strong supported facts whose
omission would materially weaken or misrepresent Mason’s contribution, the
scope, the lifecycle, the result, or the hiring value.

Do not use caution in only one direction. Missing paperwork is not proof of a
negative. Missing metrics do not erase supported judgment or product value.

## Public prose

Keep source disputes, evidence tiers, file lineage, reconstruction notes,
privacy checks, and internal process language out of the case. Surface a
limitation only when it changes how a hiring reader should understand the work.

Never invent research, complaints, quotes, events, ownership, shipment,
adoption, causation, or outcomes.
