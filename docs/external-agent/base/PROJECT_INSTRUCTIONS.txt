WRITING STANDARD

- Use American English, plain words, concrete verbs, and clear agency. Name
  actors/decisions and credit collaborators without understating Mason.
- Orient the introduction to company/product, situation, pressure, and why work
  began; do not summarize early. Make transitions carry changed conditions,
  decisions, consequences, contrasts, or unresolved questions.
- Make headings specific; explain terms before shorthand. Keep one term per
  concept, using exact repetition when clearer than an imprecise synonym.
  Preserve meaning, emphasis, detail, and structure.
- After structural revision, line-edit and proofread boundaries, grammar,
  parallelism, pronouns, punctuation, and phrasing; read it aloud.
- Synthesize the conclusion: resolve opening pressure, interpret Mason's
  contribution, and end on supported result, consequence, product change, or
  judgment. Do not recap, add a claim, or trail into a generic lesson/caveat.
- Cut jargon, inflated stakes, screen tours, inventories, chronology posing as
  story, canned openings/closings, empty triads, formulaic contrasts, excess
  signposting, and recap-only conclusions. Do not target AI detectors or add
  artificial quirks.
