# DeepL Product, Pricing, and Pricing-Page Evolution

## What can be established with confidence

From January 2023 through July 2026, DeepL’s public monetization evolved along four clear arcs.

First, the original **DeepL Pro** translator lineup at the start of 2023 was the familiar three-tier structure of **“Starter,” “Advanced,” and “Ultimate,”** plus a free tier and sales-led enterprise options. Contemporary public reporting that quoted DeepL’s page described annual per-user pricing at **€7.49 / €24.99 / €49.99** and document-translation limits of **5 / 20 / 100 files per month**, with the same per-user pricing used for team subscriptions. DeepL’s API remained separate, with **DeepL API Free** and **DeepL API Pro** sold on a usage model. citeturn14search3turn14search4turn14search8turn28search1

Second, **DeepL Write** launched as a free beta on **January 17, 2023**, then **DeepL Write Pro** launched on **April 25, 2024** as a business-grade paid writing assistant. Over time, Write moved from being introduced as its own product to being packaged in combinations with Translator and then, by the current public pricing structure, to an explicit **“Write Pro add-on”** attached to Translator plans. DeepL’s own help center now states that the current pricing page reflects a changed plan offering and that some earlier plans are no longer shown. citeturn23view0turn45search1turn45search3turn40view2turn37search0

Third, **DeepL Voice** launched publicly on **November 13, 2024** with **Voice for Meetings** and **Voice for Conversations**, initially framed as new business products rather than a mature self-serve consumer plan family. By 2026, DeepL’s help center and mobile-app purchase documentation show a mixed model: some Voice plans are sold publicly as **“Voice Starter”** and **“Voice Business”** in app stores, while **Voice for Conversations** can still be bought as a standalone sales-led plan and is also bundled into **“Voice for Meetings Business.”** citeturn43search0turn43search6turn40view2turn40view3turn40view0turn41search2turn48search0

Fourth, the public Translator ladder changed from **Starter / Advanced / Ultimate** to **Individual / Team / Business** after **June 4 and by July 21, 2025**. The same window introduced the cumulative **“Everything in …, plus”** comparison copy. By late October 2025, the former Translator + Write bundle tab had become an explicit **Write Pro add-on**. For API, current help documentation shows that **API Free** and **API Pro** are legacy purchase paths, while the current lineup is **Developer API**, **Growth API**, and **Enterprise API**; however, the exact global cutover date remains unresolved. The archive evidence is uneven but now materially stronger. Wayback preserved the **October 25, 2023** page shell but not its dynamically loaded plan grid. Arquivo.pt preserved June 2024 pricing code, while public urlscan page-content records preserved dated rendered text from 2025 and 2026. Those alternatives make it possible to bound the Translator rename, cumulative-copy redesign, and Write bundle-to-add-on transition. Locale, currency, account state, and experiment cohorts may still have produced staggered rollouts, so this report states bounded dates rather than claiming a single global launch instant. ## Product-line evolution

### Translator subscriptions

At the start of 2023, DeepL’s paid translator product was publicly organized under **DeepL Pro** with three named tiers: **“Starter,” “Advanced,” and “Ultimate.”** Public reporting in January and February 2023 quoted annual-billing prices of **€7.49**, **€24.99**, and **€49.99** respectively, and described their main visible differences as unlimited-use privileges and the monthly number of document translations included. The same articles describe the free tier as allowing only a small number of document translations, and they say team accounts used the same per-user pricing. citeturn14search3turn14search4turn14search8

DeepL’s current help-center tables make the old-to-new renaming visible. File-limit and Microsoft add-in documentation now list the old and new names side by side as **“Starter / Individual,” “DeepL Pro Advanced / Team,”** and then **“DeepL Pro Ultimate,” “DeepL Pro Business,”** which strongly suggests an intermediate migration path rather than an overnight total rewrite of plan taxonomy. Those same tables preserve important gating details: document size limits differ sharply by tier, and add-ins keep daily character caps for lower tiers while higher tiers remain uncapped. citeturn35view0turn35view1turn35view2turn35view3turn35view4

By the current public web pricing surface, DeepL’s indexed pricing snippet shows Translator sold as **“Individual,” “Team,” and “Business.”** The same current snippet still behaves like the old ladder in substance: the plan comparison surfaced in search results explicitly references the free version, then **Individual**, then **Team**, and the help docs continue to expose tier-based gating around glossary behavior, MSI install rights, network restrictions, and SSO support. citeturn25search0turn27search2turn35view5turn35view7turn36search1turn36search14

Several feature gates are visible in DeepL’s own support material. Glossary storage for **Starter / Individual** is personal-account based, while **Advanced / Team / Ultimate / Business / Enterprise** support broader glossary management. **SSO** is available only for team subscriptions with **50+ users**, and only on qualifying plans such as **DeepL Pro Advanced, Team, Ultimate, Business, Enterprise**, plus some Write and Voice team plans. **Network access restrictions** are currently limited to **Ultimate, Business, and Enterprise**. citeturn35view5turn36search1turn36search10turn36search14

Direct pricing-page records now bound the renaming. On **June 4, 2025**, the page still led with **Starter / Advanced / Ultimate**. By **July 21, 2025**, it led with **Individual / Team / Business** at the same euro prices and used the cumulative copy **“Everything in Free, plus,” “Everything in Individual, plus,”** and **“Everything in Team, plus.”** The public pricing-page transition therefore occurred after June 4 and no later than July 21, 2025. [June 4 pricing-page record](https://urlscan.io/result/01973bda-1c47-718e-bc21-1b048af6933a/content/), [July 21 pricing-page record](https://urlscan.io/result/01982c3f-d846-76bf-b548-6d12ff27a17c/content/) citeturn31view0turn35view2turn27search2

### DeepL Write

**DeepL Write** first appears on **January 17, 2023** as a free beta product, launched publicly in English and German. DeepL’s own launch post is clear that Write began as a separate AI writing tool, not as a paid Translator feature: it was introduced as a newly minted AI writing companion, free for anyone to use at launch. TechCrunch’s same-day coverage confirms the launch date and the product’s positioning as DeepL expanding from translation into writing assistance. citeturn23view0turn45search4

**DeepL Write Pro** launched on **April 25, 2024**. DeepL’s press release and blog positioning both frame it as a business-grade writing assistant with stronger security and real-time suggestions for wording, style, and tone. That launch matters monetization-wise because it marks the shift from Write as a free standalone tool into Write as a sellable premium product. citeturn45search1turn45search2turn45search3

The packaging moved through three directly supported phases. The first phase was **standalone free Write**. The second began with the April 2024 Write Pro launch and included standalone and named Translator bundle offers. Japanese launch reporting lists standalone Write Pro at **¥2,250 monthly** (**¥1,500 monthly equivalent on annual billing**), **Starter + Write Pro** at **¥3,180 monthly** (**¥2,350 annual equivalent**), and **Advanced + Write Pro** at **¥5,850 monthly** (**¥4,650 annual equivalent**). Archived June 2024 pricing code independently defines **Starter + Write**, **Advanced + Write**, and **Ultimate + Write** plan types and a bundle view. [Impress Watch launch report](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1587448.html), [archived page component](https://arquivo.pt/noFrame/replay/20240613235705id_/https://static.deepl.com/gatsby/component---src-pages-pro-js-8c1f92a1b7abc59b078f.js), [archived shared pricing code](https://arquivo.pt/noFrame/replay/20240609042712js_/https://static.deepl.com/gatsby/e98e457a04ae1e49565c830b4ef7dccd2cd31f9d-6eaafe89f7445fa67138.js) citeturn40view0turn37search0turn39search5turn39search7turn39search11

The third phase is the add-on treatment. On **October 9, 2025**, the page still exposed separate tabs for **DeepL Translator**, **DeepL Translator + DeepL Write**, and **DeepL API**. By **October 29, 2025**, the bundle tab had disappeared and the page promoted **“Enhance your writing with the Write Pro add-on.”** The web transition therefore occurred after October 9 and no later than October 29, 2025. Current help and pricing material confirms the resulting model: Write Pro can be added to qualifying Translator plans. [October 9 pricing-page record](https://urlscan.io/result/0199c773-b1c8-721b-bfc0-7b040bf86da1/content/), [October 29 pricing-page record](https://urlscan.io/result/019a2fe4-f095-7269-ba07-ab4e9803fa57/content/) citeturn40view2turn37search0

The evidence does not identify the exact day inside either rollout window, and mobile in-app bundles remain channel-specific evidence. Those limits do not weaken the narrower findings that named bundles existed at launch and that the public web page moved from a bundle tab to an add-on presentation between October 9 and October 29, 2025. citeturn40view0turn39search15turn40view2

### DeepL Voice

DeepL’s voice line formally launched on **November 13, 2024**, when DeepL announced **DeepL Voice** and its first two products: **Voice for Meetings** and **Voice for Conversations**. DeepL’s press release is the primary source for that date, and TechCrunch independently covered it the same day. The product at that point was speech-to-text translation in real time, aimed at multilingual meetings and in-person conversations. citeturn43search0turn43search6turn43search18

The launch-era sales model appears to have been business-oriented, not broad self-serve consumer pricing. Current DeepL help pages still show a partially sales-led shape: **Voice for Conversations** can be bought as a **stand-alone plan via Sales**, or accessed as part of **“DeepL Voice for Meetings Business.”** DeepL’s general plans page likewise says that standalone **Voice for Conversations** is available via Sales, while **Voice for Meetings Business** is available directly via the pricing page. That strongly implies an initial enterprise or sales-assisted phase, followed by a later move toward selective self-serve availability. citeturn40view2turn40view3

By the current period, the public naming has broadened. Help articles for Voice now list required plans including **“Voice for Meetings,” “Voice for Meetings Core,” “Voice for Meetings Business,” “Voice starter,”** and **“Voice Business.”** DeepL’s consumer-facing app-store documentation also says the mobile apps sell **“Voice Starter”** and **“Voice Business.”** In Germany’s App Store listing, those currently appear as in-app purchases at **€14.00 monthly / €143.99 annual for Voice Starter** and **€42.00 monthly / €429.99 annual for Voice Business**. Because those are app-store prices, they should be treated as mobile-IAP channel pricing, not automatically assumed to be identical to the web page. citeturn40view1turn40view0turn48search0turn48search1

DeepL’s legal terms now expose important gating details for the current Voice family. The terms describe **Starter** as including **300 minutes per month per user**, **50 online participants**, and **up to 10 participants for in-person group conversations**; **Business** includes **600 minutes per month per user**, **300 online participants**, and **up to 30 participants for in-person group conversations**. Help documentation for Voice for Meetings separately confirms support for **Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, and Zoom**, and up to **300 participants** seeing translated captions. citeturn41search2turn40view1

In **April 2026**, DeepL expanded the voice stack again with **Voice-to-Voice** and **Voice API**, moving beyond captioned speech translation into speech-to-speech and embedded enterprise workflows. A dated pricing-page record from **April 22, 2026** also places **Voice for Meetings** beside Translator and API in the plan selector, directly establishing its web-pricing-page presence by that date. The hidden Voice grid was not recovered, so its historical web prices remain unestablished. [April 22 pricing-page record](https://urlscan.io/result/019db4ba-f937-766f-a36e-c5a12470341d/content/) citeturn43search15turn23view5turn23view6turn43search1

### DeepL API

In early 2023, the public API lineup was still the familiar **DeepL API Free** and **DeepL API Pro** structure. DeepL’s Microsoft connector documentation, which reproduced DeepL’s own FAQ language, described **API Pro** as costing **€4.99 per month plus €0.00002 per translated character**, with **API Free** allowing up to **500,000 characters per month for free**. This is consistent with third-party developer documentation and 2023 ecosystem articles quoting DeepL’s help center. citeturn28search1turn28search0turn27search15

Current DeepL help documentation shows that this packaging materially changed. The current API article says the API is now sold through **Developer API**, **Growth API**, and **Enterprise API**, while **API Free** and **API Pro** “can no longer be purchased.” It also describes the new plan logic in detail: **Developer API** allows **1,000,000 characters in total**; **Growth API** includes a **fixed monthly or yearly price** with included usage, **50 million characters and 300 speech-to-text hours per month**, and charges overages above the included quota; **Enterprise API** is sales-led and based on committed usage. citeturn24view0turn27search5

The current help article also shows how DeepL’s API monetization became more product-complete over time. Legacy **API Pro** now includes, in retrospective description, not just translation, but also **DeepL API for Write**, **speech-to-text translation**, access to the **next-generation language model**, and multiple admin/API-key management features. The same article says **Growth API** includes access to Write, speech-to-text, next-generation model access, and cost-control tooling. That is a meaningful expansion from the 2023-era translation-first API packaging. citeturn24view0

The exact transition from **API Free / API Pro** to **Developer / Growth / Enterprise** is not yet reconstructable. The report must not use PricingSaaS’s tracking start as proof of a November 2025 public cutover: its current company page is not a dated November snapshot. A directly inspectable Polish DeepL pricing-page record still sold **API Free** and **API Pro** on **October 15, 2025**. Current DeepL help confirms the later lineup and says the legacy plans can no longer be purchased. The first date and channel for the new packaging remain unresolved. [October 15 Polish API record](https://urlscan.io/result/0199e88c-9a5d-7579-8931-57d905b5ee59/content/), [current DeepL API plans](https://support.deepl.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021200939-DeepL-API-plans) citeturn28search1turn24view0turn47view0

### Other launches that affected packaging

The biggest non-price packaging change in the period is DeepL’s move toward featureized, business-grade upsells layered on top of core translation. In **July 2024**, DeepL announced a **next-generation LLM** for translation quality. In present-day support copy, access to the “next-generation language model” is tied to paid API tiers, showing how a model release became part of plan differentiation rather than just a silent backend improvement. citeturn23view4turn24view0

In **March 2025**, DeepL launched **Clarify**, which it described as a new interactive feature for business translations. DeepL’s press copy framed it as personalization for the Language AI platform and as a response to demand from business customers. That matters for monetization because it reflects the company’s shift toward value-added business controls and workflow features rather than only raw translation volume. citeturn44search0turn44search2turn44search3

By **2026**, DeepL was also publicly marketing **AI agents** and **Translation Flow** within the broader platform navigation and product messaging. These are not yet reconstructed here as clean public price lines in the same way as Translator, Write, Voice, and API, but they clearly affect how the pricing page is framed: the page is no longer just “buy translation volume,” but “buy access to a larger language-workflow platform.” citeturn17view0turn30search4turn16search7

## Pricing-page structure evolution

### What the page looked like before Write and Voice changed the surface

At the start of 2023, the public commercial surface appears to have been relatively simple: a **single DeepL Pro page** centered on Translator plans, with three paid tiers and a free comparison point, plus a separate API commercial path. Contemporary public articles quoting the page all describe the pricing discussion in terms of **Starter / Advanced / Ultimate** and document-translation allowances, which is the pattern you would expect from one combined Translator grid rather than a modern multi-product stack. citeturn14search3turn14search4turn14search8

The exact Wayback capture for **2023-10-25 10:09:00 UTC** confirms that **/en/pro** was a canonical archived pricing endpoint and preserves the page shell, including “Find your perfect plan.” It does not preserve the dynamically loaded grid. Arquivo.pt and public urlscan records therefore provide the plan-level evidence used for the later dated findings. [Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20231025100900/https://www.deepl.com/en/pro), [Arquivo.pt capture](https://arquivo.pt/wayback/20240609042712/https://www.deepl.com/en/pro) citeturn33search2

### When bundles appear and when add-ons take over

The public record now bounds both the launch-era bundle phase and the later bundle-to-add-on transition.

The strongest current proof of the **add-on** phase is explicit. DeepL’s current help center says **“The Write Pro add-on can be added to any Translator plan that does not already include it,”** and the indexed pricing snippet for the current page reads **“Get more out of DeepL with an add-on… Write Pro add-on. $7.49 per user/month (billed annually).”** That is unmistakably an add-on framing, not a bundle-first framing. citeturn40view2turn37search0

At the same time, present-day customer-facing channels still preserve evidence of the prior **bundle** era. DeepL’s app-store help page lists **“Individual + Write Pro”** as a sellable in-app plan. The app-store listing itself also shows **“Individual + Write”** as a distinct in-app purchase, not merely “Individual plus optional add-on selected later.” That means the market-facing packaging has not been perfectly uniform across channels even in 2026: web now presents Write as an add-on, while mobile app stores still present at least one bundled SKU. citeturn40view0turn48search0turn48search1

Launch reporting establishes named Translator + Write bundles on **April 25, 2024**, and archived June 2024 code confirms those plan types in the pricing experience. The bundle tab remained visible on **October 9, 2025** and had become an add-on treatment by **October 29, 2025**. The exact day within that October window remains unestablished, but the web transition itself is now directly bounded. [Impress Watch launch report](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1587448.html), [October 9 record](https://urlscan.io/result/0199c773-b1c8-721b-bfc0-7b040bf86da1/content/), [October 29 record](https://urlscan.io/result/019a2fe4-f095-7269-ba07-ab4e9803fa57/content/) citeturn23view0turn45search1turn40view2turn37search0turn40view0

### When Voice starts showing up as a priced plan family

Voice followed a similar pattern of staged commercialization. At launch in November 2024, it was a new business product family rather than a mature self-serve pricing category. Current support pages still show part of that original structure: **Voice for Conversations** can be bought as a standalone sales-led plan, and it is also included inside **Voice for Meetings Business**. citeturn43search0turn40view3turn40view2

By the current period, Voice is clearly more formalized as a plan family on public channels. Help articles use a wider vocabulary of plans—**Voice for Meetings, Voice for Meetings Core, Voice for Meetings Business, Voice starter, Voice Business**—and mobile app stores sell **Voice Starter** and **Voice Business** directly. That is strong evidence that Voice moved from “new enterprise product” to a more public, SKU-based monetization model. citeturn40view1turn40view0turn48search0

A dated pricing-page record places **Voice for Meetings** beside Translator and API in the web pricing selector by **April 22, 2026**. This establishes direct web-pricing-page presence, but the hidden Voice plan grid was not recovered; app-store amounts must therefore remain separate channel evidence rather than substitutes for web prices.
