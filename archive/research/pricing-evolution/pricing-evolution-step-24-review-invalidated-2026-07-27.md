## Where the reviews agreed

### 1. The story spine works

The title is specific and active. The case has one real problem, one central
decision, a visible working sequence, Mason's collaborative contribution, and a
truthful artifact-level ending. Missing metrics do not invalidate it.

### 2. The stakes arrive too late

`By mid-2024, DeepL's pricing page had more paid-product relationships to
explain` supplies context, but the hiring reader still has to infer why repeated
plan content matters. The sharper pressure is the buying decision: if every
card repeats inherited features, the extra value of moving up a plan becomes
harder to locate.

### 3. The image pair changes more than the claimed content pattern

The repeated-list state shows four paid cards. The cumulative state adds a Free
card, changes the overall width, and revises Enterprise content as well as the
inheritance pattern. Those are genuine connected working states, but they are
not a controlled one-variable comparison. The narrative must acknowledge the
Free-card change and tell the reader to compare the paid tiers.

### 4. The same correction is narrated twice

The pair appears before the section `Restore the missing hierarchy`, with prose
already explaining that the revision made inheritance explicit. The later
section then retells the regression and correction that produced the second
image. This makes one connected turn feel like an unseen second episode.
Folding the correction into the lead-in for the revised image would remove the
collision.

### 5. The craft contribution is under-demonstrated

The draft names shorter wording, fewer broken lines, distinct benefits, and
retained administrative information, but it does not show the reader the
actual edits. The artifacts already contain usable examples, including:

- `5 editable file translations per user/month in total` becoming
  `5 file translations/month`;
- `Upload files up to 10 MB in size` becoming `10 MB maximum file size`; and
- `2,000 glossaries with 5,000 entries each, shared with your team` becoming
  `2,000 shared glossaries (5,000 entries each)`.

These examples may show collaborative editing and rationale without claiming
that Mason wrote every visible line verbatim.

### 6. The ending is accurate but defensive

`The recovered record` and the list of unsupported lifecycle and outcome claims
belong in the private controls, not in the reader-facing close. The public
candidate needs one normal sentence saying this was a working proposal, then an
ending built around the decision the sequence demonstrates.

### 7. The current evidence density favors a mini case

As written, this is one decision on one surface shown through one connected
pair. Stretching it across three explanatory sections creates repetition. A
compact two-image mini case is the stronger current shape. It could earn a
focused full-case treatment only if the exact writing changes and the
grid-versus-table system add new reader value rather than length.

## Useful differences and root judgment

Claude identified several details that Codex had not elevated on the first
read:

- The detailed comparison table is central to the stated model but is not
  shown. The repair should present the grid/table split as Mason's documented
  intent, not as a visibly completed two-surface outcome.
- The revised Ultimate card is roughly twice the Advanced price while showing
  only two added lines. That is the clearest residual risk in the cumulative
  model and a useful validation question rather than something to explain
  away.
- Several qualifiers change or disappear, including the fair-use asterisk and
  `per user/month in total`. A repair must not present every shortening as an
  uncontested improvement.
- The API tab remains visible although API is absent from the story. This is a
  low-impact artifact-context issue, not a reason to add API back into the
  narrative.

Claude also suggested that line wrapping became worse and that tooltip movement
proved progressive disclosure. Codex did not adopt either as a final finding.
The narrower five-column state wraps some lines but shortens others, so the
overall wrapping result is mixed. Tooltip icons visibly move, but their hidden
content is unavailable in this pair and should not carry a public claim.

Codex separately gave more weight to display legibility: the dense full-grid
images will need a deliberate scale, matched focus, and accessible text
treatment during any later implementation. That is Step 25 work and does not
justify creating a crop or derivative during this review.

## Consolidated verdict

**Pass with structural revision.**

This means the recovered subject and evidence are worth continuing, but the
current narrative is not approved and Step 25 must not start.

The one recommended repair is to rebuild the candidate as a compact mini case:

1. Open with the plan-comparison decision and reduce Write to one clause.
2. Keep the repeated-list image, then place Mason's cumulative-heading
   correction directly before the revised image.
3. Disclose that the revised state also adds Free and focus the comparison on
   the paid tiers.
4. Replace the repeated explanation with two or three exact copy changes and
   their intended jobs.
5. Name the sparse Ultimate card as the unresolved validation risk.
6. End with one working-proposal lifecycle sentence and the judgment the
   sequence demonstrates.

That is one structural repair, not a request for more evidence, more images, a
metric, a site change, or a broader pricing story.
